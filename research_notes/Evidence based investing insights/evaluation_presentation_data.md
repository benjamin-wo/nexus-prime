Scope note: written for Nexus (FastAPI/Postgres, cheap LLM agent, React; Singapore-based retail users holding mostly US stocks; existing feature = "chance of reaching target before stop" from historical replays + daily movers).

## 1. Backtest pitfalls and defences (overfitting, multiple testing, look-ahead, survivorship, point-in-time, purged CV, walk-forward, costs)

### Takeaway
Most backtested "edges" are selection artefacts: once you account for the number of variants tried, non-normal returns, data that was not knowable at the time, delisted stocks, and trading costs, reported Sharpe ratios/hit rates should be haircut heavily (often >50% for modest Sharpes). The defences are procedural: log every trial, deflate for trials, use point-in-time and survivorship-free data, purge/embargo overlapping labels, test walk-forward, and model costs before believing anything.

### Cited Findings
**Multiple testing / overfitting**
- Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (J. Computational Finance, 2015/2017) estimate PBO via combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV): split the trial-by-time performance matrix into S blocks, form all combinations of in-sample/out-of-sample halves, and count how often the in-sample-best configuration ranks below the median out-of-sample. — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)
- Bailey & López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality" (J. Portfolio Management 40(5), 2014, DOI 10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094): DSR corrects a Sharpe ratio for (a) the number of independent trials tried (selection bias under multiple testing) and (b) skewness/kurtosis and track-record length. — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)
- Harvey & Liu, "Backtesting" (JPM 2015): derive a Sharpe-ratio "haircut" from multiple-testing adjustments (Bonferroni, Holm, Benjamini–Hochberg–Yekutieli) given the number of tests; argue a flat 50% haircut is wrong — haircuts are nonlinear: for annualised Sharpe < 0.4 the haircut is almost always > 50%; for Sharpe > 1.0 it is at most ~25%. — [CME Group-hosted copy](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf); [Harvey slides, Jacobs Levy Center](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/3-Campbell-Harvey.pdf)
- Harvey & López de Prado comparison slides report haircut and PBO both address data mining; in their example PBO = 0.26. — [Lucky Factors presentation](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Lucky-Factors-Presentation.pdf) (secondary summary via search; numbers not independently checked)
- Haircut and PBO approaches framed as complementary; "haircut" = percentage deducted from in-sample Sharpe to estimate future performance. — [Risks (MDPI) 9(1):18](https://mdpi.com/2227-9091/9/1/18/xml)
- Harvey, Liu & Zhu, "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns" (RFS 2016): with hundreds of factors tested, a new factor should clear t > 3.0 rather than 2.0. — [DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhv059](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv059) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)
- McLean & Pontiff, "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?" (JF 2016): anomaly returns are ~26% lower out-of-sample and ~58% lower post-publication (i.e., published edges decay). — [DOI 10.1111/jofi.12365](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)

**Survivorship, look-ahead, point-in-time**
- Brown, Goetzmann, Ibbotson & Ross, "Survivorship Bias in Performance Studies" (RFS 1992): conditioning on survival can manufacture apparent performance persistence. — [DOI 10.1093/rfs/5.4.553](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/5.4.553) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)
- López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* (Wiley 2018): Ch. 7 purged k-fold CV (drop training observations whose label windows overlap the test window) and embargo (drop a buffer of observations after each test fold to block serial-correlation leakage); Ch. 11–12 backtesting dangers and combinatorial purged CV (CPCV), which yields a distribution of out-of-sample paths rather than one walk-forward path; Ch. 4 sample uniqueness/weighting for overlapping labels. — [Wiley](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Advances+in+Financial+Machine+Learning-p-9781119482086) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)

**Transaction costs**
- Novy-Marx & Velikov, "A Taxonomy of Anomalies and Their Trading Costs" (RFS 2016): many anomalies, particularly high-turnover ones, are not profitable after realistic trading costs. — [DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhv063](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv063) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)

### Inferences
- **Nexus's "chance of reaching target before stop" is itself a backtest statistic** and inherits every pitfall: (1) replays from overlapping start dates are not independent — effective sample size is far smaller than the row count (López de Prado's uniqueness logic); report confidence intervals using block bootstrap or effective-N, not naive binomial N. (2) If the replay universe is "stocks that exist today", it is survivorship-biased: stops are hit more often in names that later delisted. (3) If thresholds, lookback windows or regime filters were tuned on the same history, the number must be deflated (log every variant tried, à la DSR/PBO).
- **Baseline to beat:** for a driftless log-price random walk, P(hit upper U before lower L | price S) ≈ (ln S − ln L)/(ln U − ln L). Any displayed "chance" should be compared against this geometry-only baseline (and against an unconditional replay across all stocks); if the model can't beat it out-of-sample, the "insight" is just the target/stop distance restated. This is a cheap, honest sanity check.
- Use **walk-forward** (expanding/rolling window, re-fit only on data before each test date) as the default evaluation; use CPCV when tuning anything with many variants; keep a **trial registry table** in Postgres (every parameter set ever evaluated, with date and metric) so DSR/PBO can actually be computed.
- **Point-in-time discipline:** store `as_of` / `knowledge_date` for fundamentals (EDGAR filing date, not period-end date), use split/dividend-adjusted prices computed only with info available then, and keep delisted tickers. Earnings dates and restated financials are the most common leakage paths.
- **Costs for this user base**: commission/platform fees, FX spread SGD↔USD, bid-ask spread and slippage on stops (stops fill through gaps — replays that assume fills exactly at the stop price overstate outcomes), and 30% US dividend withholding for Singapore residents (no US–Singapore income tax treaty) should be in any return projection. (Withholding rate is general knowledge; verify with IRS NRA guidance before shipping copy.)

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the primary PDFs this session; exact formulas (DSR expected-max-Sharpe term, CSCV algorithm details) should be taken from the SSRN originals.
- No published study found specifically on survivorship bias in barrier-hit (target/stop) probabilities; the inference above is by analogy.

## 2. Evaluating probabilistic forecasts and tracking live calibration

### Takeaway
A "chance" number is only honest if it is calibrated (events called "70%" happen ~70% of the time) and the evaluation uses proper scoring rules (Brier/log score for binary outcomes, CRPS for full distributions, coverage for intervals), while rewarding sharpness subject to calibration. Live tracking is straightforward: log every displayed probability with a timestamp and resolve it later.

### Cited Findings
- Gneiting & Raftery, "Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation" (JASA 2007): defines proper scoring rules (Brier, logarithmic, CRPS, interval score); proper rules make honest reporting the forecaster's optimal strategy. — [DOI 10.1198/016214506000001437](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)
- Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery, "Probabilistic Forecasts, Calibration and Sharpness" (JRSS-B 2007): paradigm "maximise sharpness subject to calibration"; tools include PIT histograms, marginal calibration plots and sharpness diagrams. — [DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)
- Brier (1950) introduced the quadratic probability score; Murphy (1973) decomposed it into reliability, resolution and uncertainty — the basis of reliability diagrams. — [Brier 1950, Monthly Weather Review](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493(1950)078%3C0001:VOFEIT%3E2.0.CO;2) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)
- Diebold & Mariano (JBES 1995) test for whether two forecasts' losses differ significantly — usable to compare a model against a naive baseline on the same resolved events. — [DOI 10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)

### Inferences
- **Metrics for Nexus's binary "target before stop" chance:** Brier score and log loss vs. (a) the barrier-geometry baseline from section 1 and (b) the unconditional base rate; report Brier Skill Score = 1 − Brier/Brier_baseline. A skill score near zero means the number adds nothing beyond geometry.
- **Reliability diagram:** bin predictions (e.g., deciles or 0–10%, 10–20% …), plot mean predicted vs observed frequency with Wilson/bootstrap intervals; publish internally weekly. With overlapping/clustered outcomes (many users entering the same stock in the same week; market-wide crashes hitting all stops together), use cluster/block bootstrap — outcomes are highly correlated across users, so calibration can look fine for months then break in a single selloff.
- **Live tracking design:** table `forecast_log(id, user_id, symbol, shown_at, horizon, p_shown, model_version, inputs_hash, resolved_at, outcome, ...)`; a nightly job resolves outcomes from prices (target hit / stop hit / expired), then computes Brier, log loss, reliability bins, and calibration-in-the-large (mean p vs mean outcome) per model_version and per regime (VIX bucket, sector). Alert when the rolling calibration error exceeds a threshold; if it does, widen/shrink displayed bands or recalibrate (isotonic/Platt on recent resolved data). Never re-score history with a newer model.
- **Ranges/intervals:** for any price-range projection, track empirical coverage (e.g., an "80% range" should contain the outcome ~80% of the time) and interval score; CRPS if a full distribution is produced.
- **Sharpness vs honesty:** most short-horizon single-stock probabilities will legitimately be close to the base rate. Calibrated-but-unexciting is the correct output; resist UI pressure to show extreme numbers.

### Gaps
- No finance-app-specific published case study of live calibration monitoring of retail-facing probabilities was found in this session's searches.

## 3. Communicating uncertainty to non-experts; how retail investors react; how established products present projections

### Takeaway
Evidence favours expected-frequency framing ("in 100 similar past setups, about 62 hit the target first"), showing ranges/fan charts rather than point numbers, and stating uncertainty numerically (which costs little trust). Discrete ratings (stars, badges) move retail money far more than the underlying data warrants, so a single headline "chance" or score can act like a star rating. Established products wrap projections in "hypothetical, not a guarantee" language, show percentile bands, and disclose methodology — US FINRA Rule 2214 codifies this for broker-dealer tools.

### Cited Findings
**Formats**
- Gigerenzer & Hoffrage (Psych. Review 1995) — Bayesian reasoning improves markedly when information is presented as natural frequencies rather than single-event probabilities. — [DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.102.4.684](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.4.684) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)
- Spiegelhalter & Gage describe Gigerenzer's "natural frequencies" as whole-number outcomes starting from a defined population of cases; Spiegelhalter frames the Bank of England fan chart as "if economic circumstances identical to today's were to prevail on 100 occasions". — [Spiegelhalter, IRGC Symposium 2013 slides](https://irgc.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/SPIEGELHALTER-CRAG_IRGC_Symposium_2013.pdf)
- Spiegelhalter's 2025 Bank of England talk: fan chart bands at 30%, 60%, 90%, with the final 10% deliberately "not assigned – 'something else'" (explicit acknowledgement of model uncertainty). — [Spiegelhalter slides, BoE June 2025](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/events/2025/june/d-spiegelhalter-slides.pdf)
- Bank of England staff working paper (McMahon, Naylor, Rholes & Rickards, 2026), "Anchors aweigh?": fan charts are well understood and perform best at jointly conveying expectations and uncertainty; the public tends to underestimate uncertainty and fan charts mitigate de-anchoring when outcomes miss the forecast, acting as an "insurance policy" for the forecaster's reputation. (Staff paper, not Bank policy.) — [BoE working paper 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2026/anchors-aweigh-the-effect-of-communicating-forecast-uncertainty)
- Galvão & Mitchell (with Spiegelhalter co-authoring related work), Cleveland Fed working paper: multi-wave experiments on communicating UK GDP data uncertainty to the public. — [Cleveland Fed WP 21-28R](https://www.clevelandfed.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/clevelandfedsite/publications/working-papers/2022/wp-2128r-communicating-data-uncertainty-multi-wave-experimental-evidence-for-uk-gdp-pdf.pdf)
- van der Bles, van der Linden, Freeman & Spiegelhalter (PNAS 2020): 5 experiments, 5,780 participants incl. a BBC News field experiment; communicating uncertainty raised perceived uncertainty with only a small decrease in trust; numeric ranges slightly reduced trust in the number and left source trust ~unchanged, while verbal hedges ("there is some uncertainty") reduced trust more. — [Univ. Groningen record](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/the-effects-of-communicating-uncertainty-on-public-trust-in-facts/); [summary](https://blog.aiimpacts.org/p/paper-summary-the-effects-of-communicating) (one summary notes the fifth study's effects were non-significant)
- Benartzi & Thaler (Management Science 1999): showing people the distribution of long-horizon aggregated returns (rather than one-period returns) increased willingness to hold equities — display horizon changes choices. — [DOI 10.1287/mnsc.45.3.364](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.45.3.364) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)

**Retail reactions to ratings/forecasts**
- Del Guercio & Tkac, "Star Power" (JFQA 2008; Atlanta Fed WP 2001-15): event study of >10,000 Morningstar rating changes; an initial 5-star rating brings ~$26m abnormal 6-month flow (~53% above normal); upgrades attract and downgrades lose flow; it is the discrete star change itself, not the underlying performance change, that drives flow. — [Atlanta Fed WP](https://www.atlantafed.org/research/publications/wp/2001/15); [JFQA record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jfinqa:v:43:y:2008:i:04:p:907-936_01)
- Ben-David, Li, Rossi & Song, "What Do Mutual Fund Investors Really Care About?" (RFS 2022): flows track simple, salient signals such as Morningstar ratings and past returns rather than risk-adjusted alpha. — [Working paper PDF](https://foster.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/What-do-Mutual-Fund-Investors-Really-Care-About.pdf)
- Barber & Odean, "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth" (JF 2000): the most active retail traders underperformed substantially, attributed to overconfidence — a reason not to design features that encourage frequent trading. — [DOI 10.1111/0022-1082.00226](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00226) (canonical ref, not re-fetched)

**How established products present projections / regulation**
- Vanguard Capital Markets Model (VCMM) disclaimer: projections "are hypothetical in nature, do not reflect actual investment results, and are not guarantees of future results"; results "will vary with each use and over time"; the model "may be underestimating extreme negative scenarios unobserved in the historical period". Headline forecasts are medians (50th pct) with a 1-point band for equities; charts show 25–75th and 5–95th percentile bands. — [Vanguard 2025 outlook release](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/who-we-are/pressroom/press-release-vanguard-releases-2025-economic-and-market-outlook-121124.html); [Vanguard return forecasts page](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/vemo/vemo-return-forecasts.html); [Vanguard 2022 midyear outlook PDF](https://institutional.vanguard.com/content/dam/inst/iig-transformation/insights/pdf/Vanguard_economic_and_market_outlook_Midyear_2022_update.pdf)
- FINRA Rule 2214 (US broker-dealers) governs "investment analysis tools" that produce simulations/statistical analyses of the likelihood of investment outcomes (e.g., Monte Carlo); requires disclosure of criteria, methodology, assumptions and limitations, and a prescribed "hypothetical in nature… not guarantees of future results" statement (wording mirrors Vanguard's). Since Regulatory Notice 16-41, tools need not be pre-filed but FINRA must be given access on request. — [FINRA Rule 2214](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2214); [Regulatory Notice 16-41](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/16-41)
- FINRA filed SR-FINRA-2026-004 (2026) touching on projections/targeted returns; adoption status not confirmed in this research. — [FINRA filing PDF](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/SR-FINRA-2026-004-Federal-Register-Notice.pdf); [Carlton Fields commentary on earlier proposal](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/expect-focus/finra-proposes-to-permit-performance-projections-and-targeted-returns)

### Inferences
- **Copy pattern for Nexus "chance":** "Of 214 similar past setups (2005–2025, incl. delisted stocks), 58 to 70 hit your target before your stop (best estimate 64). Past patterns, not a guarantee. Doesn't include fees, FX, or gaps through your stop." — frequency format + range + N + caveats specific to the computation. Show an icon-array (100 dots) rather than a dial/gauge.
- Round displayed probabilities (to nearest 5% or show a band) when the CI is wide; never show "63.7%". Suppress the number entirely below a minimum effective sample size.
- **Avoid star-like discrete badges** ("Strong Buy", 5 stars, traffic lights) on single stocks: Del Guercio & Tkac show discrete labels drive behaviour independent of underlying information; if a summary label is unavoidable, tie it to calibrated bands and show the base rate beside it.
- Always show the **base rate / comparator** ("a random stock with the same target/stop distance: ~55%") so users see the incremental information, which is usually small.
- Use fan charts/percentile bands for any price or portfolio projection (Vanguard pattern), with the median not visually dominant, and an explicit "something else could happen" tail note (Spiegelhalter's unassigned 10%).
- Even though Nexus is in Singapore (MAS regime, not FINRA), Rule 2214's disclosure elements are a practical benchmark. Whether Nexus's features constitute "financial advice" under Singapore's Financial Advisers Act is a legal question not covered here.

- Could not verify how Morningstar's retirement Monte Carlo tools, Portfolio Visualizer, or robo-advisers (Betterment, Wealthfront, StashAway, Endowus, Syfe) word their projection disclaimers — no direct sources fetched; worth a manual review of their help pages.
- No retrieved study on Singapore retail investors specifically, nor on how retail users react to "probability of profit" numbers in brokerage apps (e.g., options POP displays).
- MAS guidance on projections/hypothetical performance in Singapore not researched.

## 4. LLMs in the loop: risks of LLM-generated market commentary and grounding patterns

### Takeaway
LLMs hallucinate financial figures at material rates even with retrieval, and when used for prediction they can leak future knowledge from pre-training (look-ahead) or be "distracted" by company familiarity. The safe pattern is compute-first, narrate-second: the LLM only phrases figures produced by deterministic code, and outputs are mechanically checked against those figures.

### Cited Findings
- FinanceBench (Islam et al., 2023): 10,231 open-book QA items on public-company filings; 16 model configurations evaluated on 150 cases (2,400 manually reviewed answers); Llama 2 more prone to hallucinating, GPT-4-Turbo more likely to refuse. — [arXiv 2311.11944](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944)
- Patronus AI (vendor launch release): GPT-4 Turbo with a retrieval system failed 81% of FinanceBench questions; with long context 21%; Claude 2 long-context 24%. Vendor-reported, small sample, 2023-era models. — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/patronus-ai-launches-industry-first-llm-benchmark-for-finance-to-address-hallucinations-301990858.html)
- A 2026 financial hallucination benchmark over 10-K knowledge graphs notes LLM judges tend to anchor on the KG formatting even when it contradicts source text — i.e., LLM-as-judge is not a reliable fact-checker. — [Domyn, June 18 2026](https://www.domyn.com/news/finreflectkg-hallubench-graphrag-hallucination-benchmark-for-financial-question-answering-systems)
- Glasserman & Lin, "Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in Stock Return Predictions Generated by GPT Sentiment Analysis" (2023): in-sample, anonymised headlines outperformed — a "distraction effect" from company knowledge mattered more than look-ahead, especially for large, well-known firms. — [arXiv 2309.17322](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2309.17322v1)
- Gao, Jiang & Yan, "A Test of Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts" (Dec 2025): propose a Lookahead Propensity (LAP) test; LLM predictive power is amplified on high-LAP firm-dates and the effect loses significance post training cutoff — evidence that apparent LLM forecasting skill in backtests is partly memorisation. — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847v1)
- Chronologically consistent LLMs (ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT): train only on data before each date; argue masking destroys information, and find look-ahead bias "modest" in their setting. — [arXiv 2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v2)

### Inferences
- **Never backtest an LLM-generated signal on dates before the model's training cutoff** and treat any such result as contaminated; for Nexus's cheap LLM, use it only for language, not prediction.
- **Grounding pattern for daily movers / trade-plan explanations:** (1) backend computes a typed "facts" JSON (price change, % move, volume vs 20d avg, chance + CI + N, earnings date, source + as-of timestamp); (2) LLM receives only that JSON and a template-constrained prompt ("use only numbers present in FACTS; if a reason for a move is not in FACTS, say the cause is unknown"); (3) post-processor extracts every number/percentage/date/ticker from the output and verifies each appears in FACTS (within rounding); reject/regenerate or fall back to a template on mismatch; (4) forbid causal claims ("fell because…") unless a news item with timestamp is in FACTS; (5) render numbers in the UI from the facts JSON, not from LLM text, where possible.
- Log prompt, facts JSON, output and validation result per message for audit; sample-review weekly.
- Don't use an LLM as the judge of factual accuracy (per the 2026 benchmark observation); use deterministic checks.

### Gaps
- No current (2026) hallucination rates found for small/cheap models on numeric financial narration specifically; Nexus should measure its own with the validator above.

## 5. Feasible data sources and costs (2026), and licensing limits on display to end users

### Takeaway
For a small team, the binding constraint is not price but **display/redistribution rights**: almost every cheap tier (Tiingo, FMP, EODHD personal, Alpha Vantage, Massive individual) is licensed for personal/internal use, and showing data to app users requires a business/redistribution plan (typically hundreds of dollars/month or quote-only). Free, display-friendly sources exist for fundamentals (SEC EDGAR, public domain) and macro/factors (FRED, French library, OSAP, AQR), but each has its own terms.

- AQR datasets: factor returns (e.g., quality-minus-junk, betting-against-beta, time-series momentum), monthly updates. — [AQR Datasets](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets)
- FRED API (St. Louis Fed): free key; macro/rates series; some series carry third-party copyright restrictions. — [FRED API docs](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended small-team stack:** one paid EOD+fundamentals vendor with a written display licence (negotiate a startup redistribution tier — Tiingo's published startup add-ons and EODHD's $399 commercial floor bracket the likely cost: roughly US$200–500/month), EDGAR for point-in-time fundamentals (use filing/acceptance timestamps as knowledge dates), French/OSAP/AQR/FRED for factor context and offline research (avoid redistributing raw series; show derived analytics), and defer options data (ORATS ~$199/mo delayed) until implied-move features are proven valuable.
- **Display vs derived:** many licences distinguish raw data display from derived analytics; showing a computed "chance" or rank may be treated differently from showing quotes — get this in writing from the vendor before launch.
- **Survivorship:** confirm the vendor includes delisted tickers in history (many cheap feeds do not by default); it is essential for section 1's replay honesty.
- **Implied move:** an at-the-money straddle price for the nearest expiry after earnings approximates the market's expected absolute move; this gives a market-implied benchmark to sit beside the replay-based chance (inference from standard options practice; not sourced this session).
- Avoid scraping Yahoo Finance or similar unofficial endpoints for a commercial app — no licence for display.
